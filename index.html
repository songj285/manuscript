My Typograpies

More than Letters: Living in a Typographic World

In My Typographies from Paul Elliman, Elliman shows that typography is much more than just printed fonts or design on a screen. He argues that our whole world is filled with readable codes and signals. To prove this, he connects many different examples from daily life such as nature and technology.

He goes from the ancient textual traditions of the Talmud to the binary form of ASCII, digital watches, shipping forecast, and the spatial of the Global Positioning System, which is GPS. Whether it is a weather report, genetic code, or a satellite location, Elliman shows that we are always reading information around us.

What resonates with me as a designer is how this text reframes my role in the creative process. Elliman suggests designers as interpreters who observe, gather, and contextualize the existing codes in the world by saying,

“Walking is reading, writing is walking”

in the last paragraph.This perspective directly connects to my own practice of collecting daily digital artifacts nowadays, such as screenshots, browser history and links, and also location pins in maps. What looks like random clutter in my phone’s photo galleries or browser tabs is actually a personal typography. It feels like my personal record of how I read my daily life. The reading My Typographies taught me that typography is not just about fixed fonts, but a living practice all over the world. It gives me a new perspective helping me find language and meaning in everyday life with small details and data around me.
